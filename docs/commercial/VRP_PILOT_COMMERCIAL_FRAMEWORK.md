# VRP Pilot Commercial Framework

## Public Information Notice

This document provides a public, non-binding overview of the commercial structure that may be used for a VRP Pilot engagement.

It is intended for informational purposes only.

It is not:

- a commercial offer;
- a quotation;
- a price commitment;
- a binding payment schedule;
- a warranty;
- a reservation of Pilot capacity;
- a promise to accept an applicant;
- a replacement for a negotiated commercial proposal;
- a replacement for a signed Master Services Agreement;
- a replacement for a participant-specific Statement of Work.

All pricing, payment, refund, tax, liability, and termination terms are determined individually and become binding only through agreements signed by the relevant legal entities.

---

## 1. Purpose

The VRP Pilot is a controlled enterprise engineering engagement for evaluating continuity-first protocol behaviour within an approved participant environment.

The commercial structure reflects the complete scope of the engagement, which may include:

- admission and qualification;
- reserved specialist engineering capacity;
- technical discovery;
- environment assessment;
- participant-specific Pilot design;
- controlled integration;
- validation execution;
- evidence production;
- engineering analysis;
- final reporting;
- agreed closure support.

A Pilot fee does not represent the purchase price of VRP, its protected runtime, its source code, or its intellectual property.

---

## 2. Nature of the Commercial Relationship

A VRP Pilot is normally structured as a milestone-based B2B professional engineering engagement.

The participant funds a defined programme of work.

The participant does not purchase:

- ownership of VRP;
- protected source code;
- unrestricted software;
- internal algorithms;
- proprietary protocol logic;
- cryptographic implementation;
- internal security mechanisms;
- permanent production rights;
- unrestricted deployment rights;
- resale rights;
- sublicensing rights;
- exclusivity.

Any rights granted to a participant are limited to those expressly stated in the signed agreements.

---

## 3. Individual Commercial Assessment

Every proposed Pilot is assessed individually.

Commercial terms may depend on:

- target environment;
- number of systems or regions;
- integration complexity;
- Pilot duration;
- required engineering capacity;
- validation scope;
- evidence requirements;
- security requirements;
- regulatory requirements;
- required support level;
- participant dependencies;
- third-party infrastructure;
- travel or on-site work;
- requested schedule;
- commercial and operational risk.

No public document establishes a universal Pilot price.

A participant receives commercial terms only after completing the required qualification process.

---

## 4. Typical Commercial Stages

A Pilot may use the following commercial stages.

### Stage 1 — Admission and Discovery

Possible activities:

- legal-entity verification;
- authorised-contact verification;
- use-case qualification;
- initial technical discovery;
- dependency identification;
- preliminary scope definition;
- engineering-capacity reservation.

Possible commercial treatment:

- advance payment;
- reservation fee;
- discovery fee;
- non-refundable committed-capacity component.

### Stage 2 — Environment Qualification

Possible activities:

- environment review;
- integration-boundary definition;
- security review;
- evidence-boundary definition;
- risk analysis;
- responsibility assignment;
- Acceptance Criteria definition.

Possible commercial treatment:

- milestone payment before work begins;
- payment following delivery;
- acceptance-linked payment;
- separately approved third-party costs.

### Stage 3 — Controlled Integration

Possible activities:

- approved integration work;
- deployment preparation;
- baseline verification;
- access validation;
- integration findings;
- readiness assessment.

Possible commercial treatment:

- advance milestone payment;
- capacity-based payment;
- approved infrastructure reimbursement;
- Change Order for expanded scope.

### Stage 4 — Validation and Evidence

Possible activities:

- execution of approved scenarios;
- collection of approved evidence;
- deviation analysis;
- evidence-integrity review;
- preliminary findings.

Possible commercial treatment:

- payment before validation execution;
- scenario-based allocation;
- reserved execution-capacity fees;
- approved external testing costs.

### Stage 5 — Final Analysis and Closure

Possible activities:

- final engineering analysis;
- Acceptance Criteria review;
- final Pilot report;
- executive findings session;
- closure support;
- next-stage recommendation.

Possible commercial treatment:

- final milestone payment;
- payment before unrestricted report delivery;
- separately agreed extended support.

## 5. Commercial Proposal

Qualified participants may receive a private, participant-specific commercial proposal.

The proposal may identify:

- contracting entities;
- engagement reference;
- proposed scope;
- proposed duration;
- commercial value;
- currency;
- milestone allocation;
- payment schedule;
- proposal validity;
- assumptions;
- exclusions;
- expected contract package.

A private proposal is prepared for a specific participant and must not be treated as a general public offer.

Publication of this framework does not require VRP to issue a proposal to any applicant.

---

## 6. Payment Principles

The final payment structure is defined in the participant-specific agreements.

Possible principles include:

1. work begins only after the required initial payment is received;
2. each milestone may require payment before activation;
3. completed work becomes earned;
4. accepted milestone fees become non-refundable;
5. reserved specialist capacity may be non-refundable;
6. third-party costs may require advance funding;
7. taxes and banking charges may be additional;
8. work outside the signed scope requires a Change Order;
9. undisputed invoices must be paid by the contractual due date;
10. access may be suspended for overdue payment.

The precise payment schedule is not established by this public document.

---

## 7. Milestone Acceptance

Each Pilot milestone should have written Acceptance Criteria.

A Deliverable may be accepted when:

- the participant provides written acceptance;
- the participant proceeds to the next milestone;
- the participant uses the Deliverable for its intended purpose;
- the contractual review period expires without valid rejection;
- a documented non-conformity is corrected.

A valid rejection should:

- be submitted in writing;
- identify the affected Deliverable;
- identify the unmet Acceptance Criterion;
- provide supporting evidence;
- remain within the agreed scope.

A new preference or newly introduced requirement is not automatically a valid rejection.

---

## 8. Technical Outcomes and Commercial Completion

A Pilot may produce one of the following technical recommendations:

- `GO`;
- `CONDITIONAL_GO`;
- `NO_GO`;
- `INCONCLUSIVE`.

These outcomes describe the result of the agreed evaluation.

They do not automatically determine whether contracted work was commercially completed.

A technically valid negative result may still represent successful completion of the Pilot when:

- the agreed scenarios were executed;
- the required evidence was produced;
- the relevant limitations were documented;
- the agreed analysis was completed;
- the required Deliverables were provided.

The Pilot funds a disciplined evaluation, not a predetermined conclusion.

---

## 9. Refund Principles

Refund rights are determined exclusively by the signed agreements.

A participant-specific contract should distinguish between:

- earned fees;
- accepted milestone fees;
- work in progress;
- reserved-capacity fees;
- approved expenses;
- non-cancellable commitments;
- third-party costs;
- unearned prepaid balances.

Possible refund principles include:

1. completed and accepted milestone fees are non-refundable;
2. completed work remains payable;
3. work in progress may remain payable;
4. reserved-capacity fees may be non-refundable;
5. approved third-party costs may be non-refundable;
6. unearned prepaid balances may be refundable after reconciliation;
7. termination for convenience does not erase payment obligations for completed work;
8. material breach normally requires written notice and an opportunity to cure;
9. security misuse may suspend or limit refund rights;
10. a full refund after work begins is not part of the standard commercial model.

No public statement creates a refund right.

---

## 10. No Outcome-Based Refund

Unless expressly agreed otherwise, a refund should not arise solely because:

- the Pilot produces a `NO_GO` recommendation;
- the Pilot produces a `CONDITIONAL_GO` recommendation;
- the Pilot is inconclusive;
- a scenario fails;
- a limitation is discovered;
- the environment is found unsuitable;
- production deployment requires additional work;
- the participant changes its strategy;
- the participant does not obtain internal approval;
- the participant expected a different business result.

The commercial question is whether the agreed engineering work was performed in accordance with the signed scope.

---

## 11. Change Control

The commercial terms apply only to the signed scope.

A Change Order may be required for:

- additional environments;
- additional regions;
- additional legal entities;
- new workloads;
- expanded validation scenarios;
- new evidence requirements;
- accelerated delivery;
- extended support;
- Participant-caused rework;
- new regulatory requirements;
- new security requirements;
- production-readiness work.

A Change Order should identify:

- the requested change;
- the reason;
- the scope impact;
- the schedule impact;
- the commercial impact;
- the security impact;
- the required approvals.

No additional work is included automatically.

---

## 12. Participant Dependencies

The commercial schedule may depend on the participant providing:

- accurate organisational information;
- authorised decision-makers;
- qualified technical personnel;
- lawful access to the approved environment;
- required infrastructure;
- required credentials;
- network permissions;
- security approvals;
- third-party cooperation;
- timely Deliverable review;
- timely commercial decisions.

Participant-controlled delay may result in:

- schedule extension;
- capacity-reservation charges;
- milestone rescheduling;
- additional fees;
- Change Orders;
- suspension;
- termination.

The final agreements should identify:

- payment currency;
- invoice schedule;
- VAT treatment;
- applicable taxes;
- withholding obligations;
- banking charges;
- payer identity;
- payment verification requirements;
- treatment of currency-conversion costs.

Tax treatment depends on the contracting entities and applicable jurisdictions.

No public document provides participant-specific tax advice.

---

## 15. Security and Compliance

Commercial approval does not override security or legal requirements.

Provider may require:

- legal-entity verification;
- authority verification;
- source-of-funds verification;
- sanctions screening;
- export-control review;
- security qualification;
- use-case approval;
- Participant personnel verification.

Provider may refuse, defer, suspend, or terminate an engagement where reasonably necessary to protect:

- legal compliance;
- intellectual property;
- Protected Technology;
- Participant systems;
- Provider systems;
- evidence integrity;
- third-party rights.

## 16. Confidential Commercial Documents

Participant-specific commercial materials may include:

- a private Commercial Proposal;
- a Master Services Agreement;
- a participant-specific Statement of Work;
- a Security Schedule;
- a Data Processing Agreement;
- approved Change Orders;
- payment instructions;
- invoice records.

They are not intended for publication in the public VRP repository.

## 17. Required Contract Package

An approved Pilot will normally require:

1. a participant-specific private Commercial Proposal;
2. a signed Master Services Agreement;
3. a signed Statement of Work;
4. required security documentation;
5. required data-protection documentation;
6. approved payment instructions;
7. any required compliance materials.

The engagement begins only after all required activation conditions are satisfied.

---

## 18. No Capacity Reservation

Publication of this framework does not reserve:

- engineering time;
- Pilot capacity;
- a position in the intake process;
- a particular schedule;
- a particular price;
- production capacity;
- exclusivity;
- future availability.

Capacity is reserved only under the conditions stated in a participant-specific signed agreement.

---

## 19. No Public Offer

This framework does not constitute an offer capable of acceptance.

Provider may:

- request additional information;
- reject an application;
- defer an application;
- revise the proposed scope;
- revise the proposed schedule;
- revise commercial terms;
- withdraw from negotiations;
- limit the number of accepted participants.

No applicant should incur costs in reliance on this framework alone.

---

All binding commercial terms must be reviewed by qualified legal and tax advisers before signature.

Particular attention should be given to:

- contracting-party identity;
- governing law;
- jurisdiction;
- milestone acceptance;
- refund treatment;
- termination rights;
- taxes;
- withholding;
- intellectual property;
- confidentiality;
- limitation of liability;
- data protection;
- export controls;
- sanctions compliance.

---

## 21. Next Step

A prospective participant should begin by submitting a structured Pilot inquiry through the current official VRP contact channel.

The inquiry should explain:

- the participant’s legal identity;
- the intended use case;
- the target environment;
- the continuity problem;
- the required evidence;
- the proposed schedule;
- the authorised decision-makers;
- the ability to complete commercial qualification.

Only qualified participants may receive private commercial terms.

---

## Final Statement

A VRP Pilot is a controlled enterprise engineering engagement.

Its commercial structure funds integration, validation, evidence production, analysis, and specialist engineering capacity.

It does not sell the VRP protocol.

It does not transfer the protected runtime.

It does not disclose proprietary implementation.

The exact price and contractual terms are determined privately for each approved participant.

**Continuity requires verification.**
