## 1. Purpose

This proposal describes the commercial basis for a controlled VRP Pilot engagement with the participant identified above.

The engagement is intended to evaluate defined continuity-first protocol behaviour within an approved environment and under agreed technical, operational, security, and evidence conditions.

The proposed fee represents the value of the complete Pilot programme, including:

- reserved senior engineering capacity;
- technical discovery;
- environment qualification;
- participant-specific Pilot design;
- controlled integration;
- deployment support;
- validation execution;
- evidence production;
- engineering analysis;
- final reporting;
- agreed post-Pilot support;
- controlled access to protected VRP evaluation capabilities.

The proposed fee is not a purchase price for the VRP protocol, protected runtime, source code, intellectual property, or production ownership rights.

## 11. Technical Pilot Results

The Pilot may conclude with one of the following technical recommendations:

- `GO`;
- `CONDITIONAL_GO`;
- `NO_GO`;
- `INCONCLUSIVE`.

These outcomes describe the result of the defined evaluation.

A `NO_GO`, `CONDITIONAL_GO`, or `INCONCLUSIVE` result does not automatically mean that VRP failed to perform the contracted services.

The commercial question is whether the agreed work was properly performed and the agreed deliverables were produced—not whether every tested environment produced a desired result.

A valid negative engineering conclusion may itself be a completed and valuable Pilot outcome.

## 12. Refund Framework

Refund rights will be governed exclusively by the signed Master Services Agreement and Statement of Work.

The proposed commercial principles are as follows.

### 12.1 Before Work Begins

If VRP terminates the engagement before beginning any contracted work and before reserving material external capacity, the participant may receive a refund of amounts paid, less:

- non-recoverable banking fees;
- approved third-party costs;
- compliance and verification costs expressly identified as non-refundable;
- other deductions permitted by the signed agreement.

### 12.2 Completed Milestones

Fees attributed to completed and accepted milestones are earned and non-refundable.

This includes milestones accepted through:

- written acceptance;
- deemed acceptance;
- use of the deliverable;
- progression to a later milestone.

### 12.3 Work in Progress

If the engagement ends while a milestone is in progress, VRP is entitled to payment for:

- work completed;
- reserved engineering capacity;
- approved expenses;
- non-cancellable commitments;
- third-party costs;
- transition and closure work.

Any remaining unearned balance will be treated according to the signed agreement.

### 12.4 Participant Termination for Convenience

If the participant terminates without a material uncured breach by VRP:

- completed milestone fees remain non-refundable;
- work-in-progress fees become payable;
- reserved-capacity charges remain payable where agreed;
- committed third-party costs remain payable;
- the participant may receive any remaining refundable unearned balance.

### 12.5 VRP Termination Without Participant Cause

If VRP terminates without participant breach and cannot provide the remaining contracted services:

- completed and accepted work remains payable;
- approved costs remain payable;
- unearned prepaid amounts associated with unperformed future work may be refunded.

### 12.6 Material Breach

A party claiming material breach must:

1. provide written notice;
2. describe the alleged breach;
3. provide supporting information;
4. allow the contractual cure period;
5. cooperate reasonably with remediation.

Refund or termination rights arise only as defined by the signed agreement.

### 12.7 Security or Misuse Suspension

Amounts are not automatically refundable when work is suspended or terminated because of:

- unauthorised access;
- reverse engineering;
- credential sharing;
- evidence tampering;
- misuse of Pilot components;
- violation of confidentiality;
- unapproved testing;
- sanctions or compliance concerns;
- participant security failures;
- participant payment default;
- material violation of the agreed use case.

### 12.8 Full Refund

A full refund after work has begun is not part of the standard commercial model.

It may apply only when expressly required by the signed agreement and only under narrowly defined circumstances.

### 12.9 No Outcome-Based Refund

No refund is due solely because:

- the participant expected a different business result;
- a particular environment is found unsuitable;
- the Pilot produces a valid `NO_GO` conclusion;
- a limitation is discovered;
- production deployment requires additional work;
- the participant changes strategy;
- the participant does not obtain internal approval;
- an unspecified expectation is not achieved.
